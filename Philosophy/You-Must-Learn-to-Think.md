---
title: You Must Learn to Think
---

In this fast paced world of changes almost every day, with paradigms shifting faster than we can name them, the instinct is to learn more. Another paper, another lecture, another repository, another framework with a clever title that just feels like the next big thing. Each of which comes with a cost, a cost which has no compensation, a cost quiet enough that you keep paying it, without ever noticing it, without ever asking yourself whether you signed up for this subscription. The purpose was never to spend time. Time spent is not the same as time invested, and hours of being logged in are not the same as understanding.

You can't connect dots looking forward, you can only connect them looking backwards. But the line leaves out the part that matters: you have to have dots, and they have to be yours. Ask yourself: where was your bookmark before it got to where it is now? You don't remember. Nobody does. It moved through a hundred pages and marked none of them, because that was never its job; it holds a place but doesn't leave a trace. The absence of dots is the reason why we are not able to define our purpose. You have some ideas, you ask Claude about it; if it agrees with you, you move ahead, and if it doesn't, you dump your idea. So ask yourself the subtle question: who is the agent here? Are you acting on your AI's ideas, or is it the other way around? The moment you realise you are running out of ideas is the moment you realise they were never yours to begin with. The trouble is you won't even notice. Most of us are stuck in the loops of prompting without even knowing the expected outcomes, and when it returns something plausible, you accept it, because plausible is indistinguishable from correct when you had no expectations of your own. Sooner than ever you would realise you never signed up for this.

Your value doesn't lie in dependence; we hold a bigger purpose, and this matters the most where it's harder to see. None of this is to undermine what AI gives us. It's genuinely exponential, and most of the time it's right, which is the trap. A thing that's right often enough stops being questioned. And the ideas worth having were never in the majority.

Chasing contributions is a loop. Chasing meaningful ones is work. The purpose of your life is not to build an application, a resume, or a bank account; it's to contribute, and it's to change the lives of those around you. The idea is not to chase completion, it is to chase meaning. There is beauty in the struggle and ugliness in the success, and everything now is built to hand you the second while skipping the first. You may feel good about finishing. But the moment you realise you left no marks to find your way back is the moment you realise you can't return to any path you took. So you wander again, find another source of gratification, then another, then another, until you reach the dead end.

> You think you must learn, you must learn to think.
