---
title: Happiness
---

Source: Naval's Podcast on Happiness

- Health is the state of being well, be it financial health, physical health, or mental health. In modern society, financial wealth can give you freedom, time, and peace, and can buy your way out of common causes of unhappiness.
- Whatever you deny yourself becomes your prison, and you cannot get out of it.
- Physical health is the foundation of everything. When you are sick or down, all other desires go away; hence it is the most important.
- Happiness is not pleasure (even the feeling after eating good food is pleasure). There is no direct definition of happiness; it is like poetry: if you fixate on the words or sentences, you learn nothing.
- Happiness is in your control. (Don't get stuck on the belief that happiness is not something that can be achieved; general contentment is in your control, and it is a skill that you identify and develop, and it slowly gives you results.) Understanding this is the most important step.
- Desire is a contract you make with yourself: you want to achieve something, and you remain unhappy until you achieve that thing. You become disturbed because you want something, and when you get it, you revert to the same stance you were in at the start. It is a delusion that achieving something will give you contentment; things look like permanent solutions, but you fall back to the same state.
- Overachievers say happiness is for stupid people, or happiness is for lazy people: if they were too happy, they would become content and lazy. The more intelligent you are, the more you start seeing the risks of all the calamities that are coming. You naturally become cynical (someone who may seem to give selfish opinions), and it is okay to not be happy.

I am unhappy because I am smart -
- You are unhappy because you know too much; you are aware of too much. You are unhappy because you are smart, and it will take more work for you to be happy.
- The good thing is that smart people are good at figuring out the truth, and the deeper you dig into the truth, the more naturally you come to peace. You can trade being smart for anything: you can figure out how to be happy, how to improve physically, how to get wealthy within your local environmental constraints. Biological constraints are much larger than what you know of. People who do drugs and psychedelics experience the full dynamic range of being human, and achieve happiness for a short duration.

I don't want to be less productive, I don't want to have less desire -
- The more content you are, the less likely you are to run out there and change the world; but at the same time, being unhappy is being inefficient. If you are a driven, unhappy person, your mind will always be racing, thinking about what happens next; you will have poorer-quality sleep; you are much more likely to dig yourself into a hole that you will then have to climb out of; you are much more likely to fall into the busy trap; you don't have peace of mind, and when it comes to making a decision, there are hundreds of threads running.
- **Happiness and contentment lead to the ability to become more effective. You stop having preconceived notions, and you can make much better decisions in life. After all, everything is about the quality of decisions. Whoever keeps making good decisions continues to obtain better results. Being busy while continuously making wrong or mediocre decisions will not lead to success. Hard work is not the solution; good decision-making is.**
- A bullish negotiation of 20% here and there will make you unhappy in the long term, and will make the other person unhappy in the long term too, so avoid that.
- Having a peaceful mind, being happier, relying less on momentary happiness, and being calm will improve your effectiveness, and as long as the increase in effectiveness is greater than the reduction in drive, you are well off.
- Do you want to be the best in the world by working the hardest, or by being the one who works less?
- Knowledge is power. Through pure knowledge we can achieve happiness, and this happiness will give us more time to read and make us more knowledgeable about how we think and how others think, making us more effective in terms of wealth and improving our judgement.
- On the other side, all pleasure or happiness creates its own offsetting pain, and the fear of losing it.
- Do not pursue pleasure for its own sake. If we pursue pleasure directly for its own sake, we land in addiction, which is difficult to get out of. Modern civilization is about lone individuals disconnected from their tribe, culture, etc. With things like social media, you fall into the trap of fake play and fake work. It's easy and it feels good; everything is available. The modern struggle is not only to stand up to these things that give you small amounts of pleasure, but also to the fact that they desensitize you to the misery of their absence; it's like a drug. If you drink alcohol, you look forward to parties; but if you decide to go to the next party without drinking, you will not look forward to it. This creates a conundrum: if I give up these sources of artificial pleasure, I bring misery upon myself. It feels miserable because you start questioning why you don't go out with your friends, why you are not social. Breaking addictions is difficult because it is not just about physically stopping something; it is about making yourself see that the lifestyle you believed in was always untrue, and you need to make yourself believe that you can be happy without that addiction. You also need to break down the beliefs in your head that make you justify the activity, and you need to get out of the fake relationships and fake activities that are held together by Instagram/social media. What if you didn't have Instagram? What if you didn't have a group? What if you didn't watch reels? Would you still be connected with the same people in the same way? Would they still know your updates without you posting stories? Would they value you enough to share their updates with you? Would you be aware of your surroundings and geopolitical situations? Would you be aware of your interests and hobbies outside of all this? The answers to these questions explain why getting out of this artificial pleasure is important, and if you still justify your addiction, then the needed realization is even bigger, and your addiction is even worse.
- What we aim to get is peace of mind; what we actually want is peace *from* mind. When you see something mesmerising, when you see a beautiful sunset, when you feel extraordinary, it is the voice in your mind that goes silent. Deep down, we are looking for peace from mind. The mind is a very useful tool, but with information overload it becomes uncontrollable, and it has become the master rather than the slave.
- Nature is brutal; modern society is a lot safer in general. It's okay to be paranoid, but not always: if you remain a pessimist, you stay alive, stay safer, and stay stressed. Pessimism is rewarded in our society, but we need to find peace.
- Modern life is more hectic, and its sources of stress are more chronic. Stress is the result of desiring two things at the same time that are mutually incompatible. "I need to relax, and I have work to do": you are in stress. "I need to go to the party, and I need to go to work": you are in stress. But when you decide on one, the overthinking goes away. Achieving peace by choosing one is difficult, but it is a skill. The goal is peace.

How do you get to peace?
- Peace is a sense that everything is fine. Peace is not something that can be achieved or worked towards directly; what you can work towards is understanding. When you understand certain things, when you learn certain things, when some things become a deep part of your knowledge, you find you are a more peaceful person. The closer you get to the truth, the closer you are to peace. The person who is the jester at a party is not peaceful inside. Wiser people are quiet because they are internally quiet.
- What society wants for you is not always what's good for you. Society is the largest group, and groups come to consensus. But if you are a smart person, you should deep down know the truths. Truths like: money won't make you content and happy. Society may believe in this lie because it keeps chasing money, and keeps chasing. Society doesn't just tell you things that are false; it also makes you feel guilty when you cross one of its boundaries. Guilt is society's voice speaking in your head.
- Peace is the search for truth. When you see something strongly enough, when you know things deep down, you start detaching from the things that bring discontentment. When you see the truths, when you see some facet of yourself that you cannot unsee and cannot forget, when you see some facet of someone that you cannot forget.
- If you want peace, you need to give up self-conflict. Understanding is what will make you tell the truth, and the path towards truth is the path towards peace.
